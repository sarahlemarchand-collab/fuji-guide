Top of camera
Image 1 — top view
Shutter speed dial — top right
The big disc. The marker is the tiny black dot on the body at the top edge — whichever number sits under it is your setting. The red A needs to be under that dot for Aperture Priority.
The sequence around the disc: B T 1 2 4 A 8 15 30 60 125 250 500 1000 2000 4000
A
Aperture Priority — use this!
You set aperture with the front dial. Camera picks shutter speed automatically.
M
Full Manual
Front dial = aperture. Rear dial = shutter speed. You control both.
SR
SR Auto / Scene modes
Camera controls everything. Front dial gives you scene options — not aperture. Avoid this.
Drive dial — top left
Must be on S for normal shooting. If it's on Adv or P the front dial gives scene options instead of aperture!
S
Single shot — use this normally
One photo per press. Normal everyday shooting mode.
CL / CH
Continuous low / high burst
Hold button for rapid-fire shots. Good for kids running or action.
ADV
Advanced filters
Fun effects (toy camera, miniature). Front dial becomes scene picker here — not aperture!
ON/OFF + ISO switch
ON
OFF
Power switch
Collar around the shutter button. Twist to turn camera on or off.
AUTO
ISO switch — leave on AUTO
The small flip switch. AUTO = camera picks sensitivity. The dot position unlocks manual ISO. Leave on AUTO for now.
Exposure compensation dial — right of hot shoe
The dial with -3 · -2 · -1 · 0 · +1 · +2 · +3. Keep on 0 normally. Photo too dark? Turn to +1. Too bright? Turn to -1. Easiest dial on the camera!

Front & lens
Image 2 — front view
MCS dial — front left of body
Controls your focus mode. Three positions:
S
AF-S — Single focus
Locks focus when you half-press the shutter. Use this most of the time — portraits, buildings, still subjects.
C
AF-C — Continuous focus
Keeps tracking a moving subject. Great for kids running or people walking towards you.
M
MF — Manual focus
You turn the focus ring on the lens yourself. Advanced — leave on S for now.

Your lens — XF 15–45mm
This lens has no aperture ring — that's normal! The only ring is the zoom ring. Aperture is controlled electronically via the front command dial.
ZM
Zoom ring — outer ring
Twist to zoom in (45mm) or out (15mm). The wider ribbed ring.
f/
Aperture — via front dial on body
Ranges from f/3.5 (most background blur, most light) to f/22 (everything sharp). No physical ring on this lens.

Top buttons row
🗑
Delete button
Deletes photos in playback. Asks you to confirm first.
▶
Playback button
Shows your taken photos. Half-press shutter to return to shooting.
VM
View Mode
Switches between viewfinder, back screen, or auto (screen turns off when eye is near).

AE-L — locks brightness
Hold to freeze the exposure setting. Point at a wall, lock brightness, then reframe at your subject. Stops camera from changing brightness when you move.

Between AE-L and AF-L. Your thumb sits here naturally. In A mode = exposure compensation. In M mode = shutter speed.
AFL
AF-L — locks focus
Hold to freeze focus. Focus on a face, hold AF-L, reframe your shot nicely, then shoot. Focus stays on the face even though you moved.

Your best friend! 16 shortcuts in one screen — film simulation, white balance, ISO and more. Much faster than the main menu.

Manual settings
Aperture, shutter, focus
What is aperture?
Imagine a door. Wide open = lots of light but things far away look blurry. Tiny crack = less light but everything sharp. Aperture is the camera's door.
f/3.5
Wide open — most light, blurry background
Great for portraits. The background goes soft and dreamy (bokeh). This is the widest your 15-45mm lens opens.
f/5.6
Middle — good all-rounder
Some background blur, good sharpness. Use for street and everyday photos.
f/8
Small — everything sharp
Near and far all in focus. Perfect for buildings and architecture.
How to change it: Turn the front command dial (index finger, front of camera). Shutter dial must be on A or M.
What is shutter speed?
A tiny curtain inside the camera opens and closes every time you shoot. Fast = freezes motion. Slow = blurs motion into streaks.
1/500
Fast — freezes action
Footballs, running kids, moving people all look frozen sharp.
1/125
Normal — everyday shooting
Good for most situations. People standing still, portraits, buildings.
1/30
Slow — light trails and blur
Moving things streak. Car headlights at night become glowing lines. Use a tripod!
How to change it: In A mode the camera sets it automatically. In M mode use the rear command dial (thumb, back of camera).
Best mode to start — Aperture Priority
1
Set shutter dial to A
Big disc on top right. Red A must be under the black marker dot.
2
Set drive dial to S
Top left dial to S (single shot). If it's on ADV the front dial won't control aperture!
3
Turn the front dial
Watch the f-number change on screen — f/3.5 → f/5.6 → f/8. You're controlling aperture!
4
Shoot!
Half-press to focus (green box appears), full press to take the photo.
Focus modes — MCS dial on front
AF-S
Single — use for portraits
Locks focus on half-press. Also turn on Face/Eye Detection in menu for automatic eye tracking.
AF-C
Continuous — use for moving subjects
Keeps tracking as long as you hold the shutter half-pressed.
Touch AF
Touchscreen AF — set switch to AF
Tap a face on screen to move the focus point there instantly.

Background blur tip — for all styles
Your 15-45mm lens opens to f/3.5 max — you won't get super dreamy bokeh yet. To maximise blur: zoom to 45mm, set f/3.5, and get physically close to your subject. That's the most this lens can do. For more blur later, look at the Fujinon XF 35mm f/2 — affordable and incredible.
